A K that says what the product does

How the Kampalo mark, the Kai mark and the line Ask. Automate. Grow. were arrived at — what was tried, what was rejected, and the rules that keep the set consistent from a 16px favicon to a client-facing report cover.

Where we started

Kampalo already had a geometric K in a steel-to-navy gradient, sitting at roughly 25px in the product sidebar. The brief kept the letter and the navy family, and ruled out the obvious category props: no charts, no megaphones, no growth arrows, nothing that looks like Google or Meta, and no thin inner detail that dies in a sidebar.

Four constraints did most of the work. The mark has to read at 16–24px in a sidebar and a browser tab. It has to survive one-colour print at 32px on a report header. It has to hold its ground next to an agency's own uploaded logo without competing. And it has to work light-on-navy, because navy-on-navy fails in dark mode.

Eleven rounds, five arguments

Each round settled one question, and each rejected mark taught the next one something.

1 · Geometry — three separated planes

Handsome at 132px, and the gaps read as "separate accounts". But the gaps are the first thing to close up at 16px, and the mark said nothing a hundred other SaaS K's don't. Set aside: pattern without an argument.

2 · Meaning — arms that converge

Tapering the arms into a single throat at the stem gave the letter a direction and an idea: many channels, one column. This is the geometry that survived to the end. What it still lacked was any sense of the outcome.

3 · Category — the funnel and the lead

A wide mouth, a narrow spout, and one detached square dropping out of it. The funnel is the only marketing metaphor that is structural rather than decorative — it can be built out of the letter instead of placed beside it. The loose square became the most useful element in the system.

4 · Enterprise — flat, contained, one colour

Dropping the gradient, tightening the splay and adding margin inside the mark did more for credibility than any new shape. The ruled square is kept as a document variant: it gives Kampalo a fixed footprint beside a client's uploaded logo.

5 · Synthesis — one closed path

The funnel redrawn as a single outline: mouth and spout separated by one shoulder on the stem, plus the detached lead square. Nothing to break in print, one path to maintain, and the meaning intact at 16px.

Two further rounds tested AI cues — a context satellite, a two-pronged connector port — and both made the platform mark busier for a claim the mark cannot carry alone. That produced the decision that shaped the rest of the system: the platform mark stays straight-faced, and the AI gets its own mark.

The mark

Drawn on a 64-unit grid. The stem occupies units 8–20, the mouth opens across the full width above the midline, and the spout leaves at half the mouth's thickness. The lead square sits five units clear of the spout — close enough to belong to it, far enough to read as separate at small sizes.

Everything is straight-edged except that square, whose 2-unit radius is the only soft corner in the platform identity. That is deliberate: it is the element that moves, changes colour and carries state.

Kai

A prompt caret above an upturned arc: a face with no eyes, so it stays software rather than mascot. Same 64 grid as the K, opposite temperature — rounded terminals, generous corner radius, purple instead of navy. The caret is the smart half, the arc is the friendly half, and neither depends on colour to work.

Kai is a sub-brand, not a variant of the logo. It appears in the assistant panel, on agent badges, and standalone if Kai ever ships as an API. The contrast between the two marks is the point: a person inside a system.

The line

Seventeen candidates were tried across three axes — consolidation ("one dashboard for every ad account"), stack position ("the intelligence layer for ad spend") and outcome ("ads that grow your business"). The consolidation lines described a feature, and the stack-position lines described a category. Ask. Automate. Grow. won because it describes the sequence a user actually moves through: they ask Kai a question, the pipeline runs, the business grows. It is three words, so it survives the 180px email lockup, and it does not promise autonomy the product has not shipped.

Plus Jakarta Sans throughout. The wordmark is weight 700 in all caps at +0.10em tracking; the descriptor is weight 600 in all caps at +0.14em. Kai's own name is set in weight 800 with tight tracking, sentence case — the only place the identity uses lowercase, and another way the agent reads warmer than the platform.

Rules

  • Minimum icon size 16px. Clear space around the mark equals half its height.
  • Hide the descriptor below 150px lockup width — mark and wordmark only.
  • On navy, the lead square may take #8B6EFF. Everything else stays one colour.
  • No gradient on the mark. The steel-to-navy gradient belongs to email headers and buttons.
  • Never stretch, outline, rotate or add effects to the lockup; never redraw the K in another weight.
  • On a co-branded report, use the sealed variant so the client's uploaded logo cannot outrank it.
