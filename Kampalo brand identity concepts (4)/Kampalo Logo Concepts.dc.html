Round 11 — growth + automation

Ads that grow the business, run by AI

Growth is the outcome, automation is the method, and the AI is what makes the second one credible. Order matters: outcome first, mechanism second — a line that leads with the technology sells to nobody outside engineering.

11a
KAMPALO Ads that grow your business, automated
11b
KAMPALO AI that runs your ads and grows your business
11c
KAMPALO Growth ads, automated end to end
11d
KAMPALO Every ad platform, automated by AI
11e
KAMPALO Ask. Automate. Grow.
11f
KAMPALO AI-run ads. Real growth.
Which one, and why

11a is the pick: growth is the promise, "automated" is the differentiator, and it reads the same to an agency owner and an in-house marketer. 11b is the fullest claim and the right line for a hero headline rather than a lockup — it runs past the 180px email width. 11e is the most memorable and the most agency-brochure; use it as a section header, not the descriptor. One caution, the same one as before: "automated" and "runs your ads" commit you to Kai taking action. If Kai only answers questions today, 11a is defensible and 11b is not.

Which one, and why

10a is the pick. It is the shortest way to say both halves of the claim — every platform, one pipeline — it works for an agency running twelve clients and a business running one, and it stays true when TikTok and Shopify land. 10b spells out the pipeline for a landing page where you have room to be explicit. 10d is the boldest and the most platform-like, but it describes what you accept rather than what the customer gets. 10c only works if creative briefs really do start inside Kampalo.

Round 3 — the marketing job, inside the K

Spend in, leads out

The brief rules out charts, megaphones and growth arrows, so the category has to be carried by the letter's own structure rather than a bolted-on symbol. Each of these three encodes one step of the ad job — funnel, click, reach — and still reads as a K at 16px.

3a
Funnel
The K's arms are a funnel: a wide mouth at the top, a narrow spout below, and one detached square dropping out of it — the qualified lead. Top of funnel to conversion, read left to right, no chart required. The dot is also the piece that can turn green for a live campaign.

A caution worth stating: a mark cannot say "ads, marketing and leads" on its own — no logo does. Google, Meta and HubSpot all use abstract marks; the category comes from the wordmark next to it and the product behind it. What a mark can do is hold one idea consistently. If you want the category read explicitly, the cheaper move is a tagline lockup — Kampalo · one dashboard for every ad account — under any of these marks, rather than pushing more meaning into 16 pixels.
